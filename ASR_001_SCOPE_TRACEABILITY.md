## 3. Exclusion Traceability

| Exclusion | Evidence basis |
| --- | --- |
| Universal sport definition | BC-002 and disputed computational contests show that ASR-001 cannot settle all sport ontology questions |
| Minimum embodiment threshold | BC-011, BC-012, and BC-018 support channel diversity without a defensible universal quantity |
| Total score or maturity level | Twenty cases remained discriminating through qualitative profiles and explicit uncertainty |
| Product ranking | Corpus cases are object- and configuration-specific rather than market comparisons |
| Automatic category certification | BC-005, BC-007, BC-009, BC-014, and BC-018 retain partial, product-specific, or unresolved relations |
| Clinical efficacy | BC-016 separates system classification from clinical-effectiveness evidence |
| Remote technical tolerances | BC-015 records remote synchronous integrity as not evidenced rather than inventing thresholds |
| Final BCI membership | BC-018 supports intentional biological agency while athletic embodiment remains unresolved |
| Safety certification | Safety disclosures appear across AS³ but the corpus does not establish certification methods |
| Federation recognition | Institutional recognition and Altis category analysis remain separate axes |

## 4. Traceability Rule for Future Requirements

A later candidate clause is traceable only when it identifies:

- one approved scope domain;
- the problem the clause addresses;
- supporting evidence or a declared governance dependency;
- the profiled object and applicability condition;
- a verification approach;
- exclusions and uncertainty affected by the clause.

This rule does not itself create a normative clause.
