AltisSports

Reference Infrastructure for Spatial Sport

«What becomes of sport when the space that contains it becomes computational?»

AltisSports is governed reference infrastructure for describing, inspecting, citing, and reviewing spatial athletic systems across physical, virtual, distributed, human, machine, and hybrid environments.

It is not a sports publication, a product-ranking system, or a certification authority.

It is an attempt to build the language, evidence structures, reference architecture, machine-readable objects, and review discipline needed to examine a field whose boundaries are becoming increasingly difficult to describe with conventional sporting or technological categories alone.

---

Why Altis

The name is structural, not decorative.

The Altis at ancient Olympia was more than a venue for athletic action. It was a bounded civic and sacred environment in which competition, rules, spectatorship, ritual, identity, authority, and collective memory were held together.

Sport did not exist independently of the environment that contained it.

Today, that environment is changing.

Athletic action may pass through physical rooms, computational arenas, sensors, remote participants, adaptive equipment, software rules, automated officiation, machine inference, virtual spectatorship, and distributed networks.

The arena can now sense.

It can compute.

It can mediate.

It can constrain.

It can measure.

It can compare.

It can officiate.

That changes the analytical problem.

«The ancient Altis contained sport in physical space.
AltisSports examines how sport is described, evidenced, and governed when space itself becomes computational.»

History is not used here as ornament. It provides the structural analogy from which the reference problem begins.

---

When Space Becomes Computational

A headset does not create a new sporting category merely because it renders three-dimensional space.

A sensor does not make two performances comparable merely because both produce the same metric.

A virtual environment does not become an athletic arena merely because competition occurs inside it.

Automation does not become human performance merely because a human designed the system.

Immersion is not embodiment.

Mediation is not spatiality.

Technology is not category membership.

Training is not automatically sport.

And a provider's use of the word spatial is not evidence that spatiality is operationally meaningful.

The deeper question is:

«What operational relationship exists between human performance and computationally mediated space, and what evidence allows that relationship to be inspected?»

This is the problem space AltisSports calls Spatial Sport.

---

Spatial Sport

Spatial Sport is the reference field concerned with the relationship between sport, athletic systems, and computationally mediated space.

Its core problem is not whether a system uses XR, three-dimensional graphics, sensors, digital representation, or another fashionable technical layer.

The problem is whether computationally mediated space has an evidenced operational relationship to performance, training, measurement, comparison, officiation, participation, or another relevant function — and what type of system that relationship creates.

AltisSports therefore distinguishes among objects that are often collapsed together:

- athletic contests;
- spatial athletic systems;
- training and simulation systems;
- computational contests;
- measurement and officiation systems;
- support systems;
- spectator and broadcasting systems;
- products, platforms, events, and organizations.

A finding about one object must not silently become a finding about another.

That is the object-lock principle.

---

The Missing Reference Layer

Spatial Sport is emerging through disconnected products and practices:

- immersive athletic training;
- motion-based competition;
- mixed-reality exercise;
- remote embodied participation;
- distributed arenas;
- sensor-mediated performance;
- computational officiation;
- adaptive and assistive systems;
- spatial broadcasting and spectatorship;
- human–machine performance systems.

Products exist.

Platforms exist.

Competitions exist.

Sensors exist.

Research exists.

What remains fragmented is the reference layer between them.

Without that layer:

- simulation can be confused with sport;
- immersion can be confused with embodiment;
- provider claims can be mistaken for independent fact;
- measurements can be compared without valid comparability conditions;
- automated execution can be confused with live Performance Agency;
- rules implemented in code can become difficult to inspect;
- accessibility can be treated as an edge condition rather than part of system architecture;
- data and inference can be mistaken for direct observation;
- institutions can struggle to compare systems whose technical vocabulary is incompatible.

AltisSports is being built to make those distinctions explicit, traceable, and inspectable.

AS³ — Altis Spatial Sport Stack

AS³ is the provisional reference architecture used to locate where a spatial athletic system enables performance, carries rules, produces measurements, and can lose integrity.

It is an inspection architecture, not a maturity model.

Its layers are not levels.

Their presence does not produce a score.

L1 — Performance Agency

Who or what performs, and how live human skill affects the relevant performance window.

L2 — Performance Interface & Embodied Demand

How human skill enters the system and what bodily, physiological, cognitive, sensorimotor, adaptive, or assisted channels materially contribute.

L3 — Arena & Spatial State

How physical, computational, unified, and distributed arenas are represented and synchronized.

L4 — Constraint & Rule Execution

Where rules reside and how textual, human, equipment, sensor, geometry, or code-based rules become operative.

L5 — Sensing, Tracking & State Estimation

How participant, equipment, arena, and event state are observed, measured, estimated, or inferred.

L6 — Measurement & Comparability

How observations become metrics and under what conditions comparisons are defensible.

L7 — Officiation & Outcome

How events become decisions, penalties, rankings, corrections, appeals, or results.

L8 — Presence & Participation

How performers, officials, coaches, spectators, and remote actors perceive, communicate, and affect the system.

L9 — Safety, Accessibility & Human Limits

How physical, physiological, cognitive, adaptive, and social participation conditions are disclosed.

L10 — Governance, Evidence & Change

How authority, provenance, accountability, versioning, correction, data rights, conflicts, and external inspectability are governed.

Across those layers, AS³ also examines conditions such as agency handoff, distributed arenas, comparability, accessibility, privacy, security, intentional biological control, and operational spatial integration.

A Reference Must Survive Skeptical Inspection

AltisSports does not treat authority as something that can be declared into existence.

The governing model is:

«attributed, falsifiable, machine-readable artifacts → citation and reliance → reference authority»

Every material claim is expected to make its epistemic status visible.

Claim classes

- C1 — Directly Documented Fact
- C2 — Multi-Source Factual Synthesis
- C3 — Attributed External Claim
- C4 — Altis Analytical Interpretation
- C5 — Provisional Category Proposition
- C6 — Commercial or Forecast Claim

Provider claims must remain distinguishable from Altis interpretation.

Uncertainty must remain visible.

Provisional theory must remain provisional.

Historical material must not silently become current.

A source must support the sentence for which it is cited.

And AI output is not treated as evidence.

---

Citability Is Infrastructure

A reference becomes useful when another human or machine can cite it without guessing what it means.

For that reason, externally citable AltisSports objects require:

1. a stable identifier whose meaning cannot silently change;
2. an explicit claim class;
3. inspectable attribution and provenance;
4. an explicit license;
5. a temporal status;
6. a falsifiability or correction path;
7. preservation of superseded identity rather than identifier reuse.

This is designed for more than human reading.

Researchers should be able to inspect the evidence.

Engineers should be able to resolve structured objects.

Analysts should be able to distinguish fact from interpretation.

Journalists should be able to trace a statement to its status and source.

AI systems should be able to ingest licensed, versioned, provenance-rich objects without silently converting provisional interpretation into fact.

The Descriptive Atlas

The first Altis Atlas layer is deliberately descriptive.

It can state:

- what a system is;
- what a provider claims;
- what the evidence supports;
- which dimensions are present;
- what remains unknown or unresolved;
- which source object governs the record.

It does not currently issue:

- rankings;
- quality judgments;
- maturity scores;
- product recommendations;
- certification;
- conformance verdicts.

The Atlas exposes what the governed corpus knows.

It does not manufacture what the corpus does not know.

ASR-001

ASR-001 — Spatial Athletic System Evidence Profile is an unpublished Working Draft concerned with documenting one bounded, versioned system configuration.

Its purpose is evidence inspectability.

It is not a mechanism for declaring that the underlying system is:

- a sport;
- safe;
- clinically effective;
- certified;
- superior;
- federation-recognized;
- adopted by industry.

Candidate requirements address areas such as:

- object identity and scope;
- system context and roles;
- Performance Agency;
- performance interfaces;
- arena topology;
- constraints and rules;
- sensing and evidence states;
- measurement and comparability;
- officiation and outcomes;
- spatial integration;
- accessibility and responsibility;
- provenance and governance;
- correction and supersession;
- machine-readable representation.

Its status remains explicitly provisional.

Multidisciplinary by Design

Spatial athletic systems cross professional boundaries that conventional product categories often keep separate.

AltisSports is therefore designed to be inspectable from multiple disciplines.

Spatial systems engineering

Topology, interfaces, sensing, state estimation, distributed environments, spatial integration, extensibility, and failure behavior.

Sport governance and integrity

Rule authority, operative execution, officiation, accountability, correction, appeals, recognition boundaries, and institutional responsibility.

Performance measurement

Calibration, metrics, uncertainty, comparability, attribution, outcome structure, and version-dependent validity.

Accessibility and adaptive sport

Adaptive equipment, diverse bodily interfaces, supervised participation, classification context, accessibility disclosure, and avoidance of universal-body assumptions.

Evidence and data governance

Provenance, temporal status, uncertainty, telemetry, identity, physiological data, privacy boundaries, correction history, and machine validation.

Research methodology

Construct validity, evidence classification, boundary cases, falsifiability, provisional propositions, and resistance to hidden category scoring.

No one discipline is allowed to silently stand in for the others.

What AltisSports Does Not Claim

AltisSports is not:

- a sports news publication;
- a product certification authority;
- a conformance service for underlying systems;
- a sports-recognition body;
- an adopted or recognized standard;
- a universal definition of sport;
- a ranking system;
- a maturity ladder;
- a spatiality score;
- a safety standard;
- a clinical standard;
- a claim of industry adoption;
- a claim of external endorsement.

It does not claim ownership of the descriptive words Spatial Sport.

Its defensible position must come from the quality of its distinctions, evidence, architecture, identifiers, datasets, correction discipline, and eventual independent use.

---

Independence by Architecture

AltisSports is intended to remain independent at the level that matters most: the evidence itself.

Payment, sponsorship, membership, licensing, or commercial relationships may not alter:

- evidence thresholds;
- claim classifications;
- limitations;
- methodology;
- correction rights;
- conclusions.

Reference authority cannot be purchased.

A favorable result cannot be purchased.

A citation does not become true because it is commercially useful.

The asset becomes strategically important only if its work remains useful even to someone who disagrees with its conclusions.

---

Falsifiability

AltisSports is designed to be correctable.

The Category Thesis must be revised if later evidence shows that its distinctions fail.

AS³ must be revised if its layers cannot be distinguished operationally, cannot be tied to observable evidence, or encourage misleading scoring.

Individual records must expose what evidence would change their interpretation.

Corrections must remain traceable.

«A reference that cannot be corrected cannot become a reference.»

Governing Principle

AltisSports is not trying to become difficult to bypass by declaring itself authoritative.

It is trying to become difficult to bypass by making its work:

specific enough to inspect,
structured enough to reuse,
stable enough to cite,
transparent enough to challenge,
and honest enough to correct.

«Authority is not announced before the work.
It accumulates through work that others can quote, verify, and correct.»
