# AltisSports Asset Thesis

**Version:** 0.1  
**Status:** Foundation Draft

## 1. Asset Definition

AltisSports is a governed reference and intelligence system for Spatial Sport.

It is not conceived as a sports news publication, VR game studio, consumer fitness application, metaverse destination, or domain-sale landing page. It is intended to become infrastructure through which spatial athletic systems can be described, compared, evaluated, designed, and governed.

## 2. Conceptual Origin

The Altis at ancient Olympia was not merely a sporting venue. It was a bounded civic and sacred environment in which athletic action, rules, spectatorship, ritual, identity, and collective memory were held together.

Spatial computing changes the arena. Athletic action may now cross physical rooms, virtual environments, sensors, remote participants, computational rules, and synthetic spectatorship.

AltisSports extends the historical logic of the arena:

> The ancient Altis contained sport in physical space. AltisSports defines how sport is contained, measured, and governed in spatial systems.

History is not used as decoration. It supplies the structural analogy that explains why the asset exists.

## 3. Category Problem

Spatial Sport is emerging through disconnected products and practices:

- immersive athletic training;
- motion-based competition;
- mixed-reality exercise;
- remote embodied play;
- spatial sports broadcasting;
- digital arenas;
- simulated officiation;
- sensor-mediated performance measurement.

The market lacks a sufficiently stable shared architecture for distinguishing, comparing, and governing these systems.

Without such an architecture:

- simulation may be confused with sport;
- immersion may be confused with embodiment;
- device output may be treated as comparable without calibration;
- physical effort may be claimed without evidence;
- virtual spectacle may be confused with athletic competition;
- platform rules may be hidden inside proprietary systems;
- institutions may be unable to compare vendors coherently.

## 4. Asset Function

AltisSports should produce governed category intelligence through:

- first-principles research;
- a category ontology;
- AS³, the Altis Spatial Sport Stack;
- ASR, the Altis Spatial Reference family;
- a source-disciplined market and technology atlas;
- machine-readable definitions and datasets;
- evidence-linked profiles;
- comparison and readiness tools;
- institutional briefs and licensed intelligence.

## 5. Primary Operating Audience

Organizations and professionals responsible for defining, designing, evaluating, procuring, governing, researching, or investing in spatial sports systems.

Representative users include:

- sports-technology product leaders;
- XR and spatial-computing teams;
- sports federations and leagues;
- performance scientists;
- immersive media and broadcasting teams;
- motion-tracking and analytics providers;
- sports integrity and governance professionals;
- researchers in sport science and human-computer interaction;
- investors and strategic analysts.

## 6. Revenue Function

AltisSports must be capable of generating respectable recurring income without weakening its authority.

The preferred model is:

> Open reference authority + paid structured intelligence.

Possible revenue layers include:

- professional intelligence memberships;
- paid market briefs;
- licensed atlas data;
- API access;
- evidence profile services;
- institutional readiness assessments;
- ASR-based commercial integrations;
- research memberships and briefings.

Advertising-led publishing, paid rankings, unmarked sponsored judgment, speculative tokens, and thin affiliate pages are outside the preferred model.

## 7. Strategic Buyer Logic

The strongest future buyer is an organization that already participates in Spatial Sport but lacks authority over its reference architecture.

Potential buyer classes include:

- immersive sports technology companies;
- spatial-computing platforms;
- sports data and tracking companies;
- sports media and rights holders;
- stadium and fan-experience technology providers;
- sports federations or institutional innovation bodies.

A strategic buyer should perceive several losses if it does not acquire the asset:

- loss of the category-defining domain;
- loss of accumulated terminology and standards;
- loss of structured datasets;
- loss of independent authority;
- loss of recurring revenue and institutional relationships;
- risk that a competitor acquires the reference position.

## 8. Boundaries

AltisSports will not initially:

- certify products;
- publish universal scores;
- claim final authority over what counts as sport;
- operate a pay-to-rank directory;
- generate large numbers of thin pages;
- publish unsourced market claims;
- present provisional theory as settled fact;
- build a complex tool before its logic is documented.

## 9. Success Condition

AltisSports becomes strategically valuable when its absence from a serious inquiry into Spatial Sport becomes noticeable.

That requires use, citation, structured data, defensible definitions, independent trust, and operational outputs—not merely a polished website.
