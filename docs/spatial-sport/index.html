Category thesis · provisional

Spatial Sport

The reference field concerned with the relationship between sport, athletic systems, and computationally mediated space. The category name remains descriptive. AltisSports does not claim ownership of the words.

The core problem

The problem is not whether a system uses XR, three-dimensional graphics, sensors, digital representation, or another fashionable technical layer.

The problem is whether computationally mediated space has an evidenced operational relationship to performance, training, measurement, comparison, officiation, participation, or another relevant function — and what type of object that relationship creates.

A Spatial Athletic System is a system in which live human Performance Agency and an evidenced embodied performance channel are operationally connected to computationally mediated space.

This is a provisional research definition. The minimum embodiment threshold and normative integration requirements remain unresolved. Source: Category Thesis v0.2.

Necessary distinctions

Immersion ≠ embodiment

Immersion concerns perceptual presence. Spatiality concerns operational function. High presence may coexist with weak spatial-athletic integration.

Mediation ≠ spatiality

Vehicles, prostheses, controllers, ECUs, sensors, and remote interfaces can mediate performance without making the arena computationally constituted.

Technology ≠ category membership

A product, platform, headset, trainer, tracking system, or visual overlay does not inherit Spatial Sport status from branding.

Spatial ≠ virtual

A virtual environment may remain representational, or may host a computational contest, without an evidenced athletic performance channel.

Training ≠ automatically sport

The same technical stack may support training, rehabilitation, simulation, or championship competition. Classification depends on the object and event structure, not scenery.

Object-lock

A finding about an activity, product, event, organization, or support system must not silently become a finding about another object.

Why the field and the core class are separated

Spatial Sport cannot safely name every product or experience that touches sport and spatial technology. The field contains several object types:

  • athletic contests;
  • spatial athletic systems;
  • training and simulation systems;
  • computational contests;
  • measurement and officiation systems;
  • support systems;
  • spectator and broadcasting systems;
  • products, platforms, events, and organizations.

Spatial Athletic Contest

A Spatial Athletic System operating under identifiable constraints, comparison conditions, and a performance-dependent open outcome.

Spatial Support System

A system that supports training, assessment, rehabilitation, measurement, officiation, spectatorship, broadcasting, social participation, or research. It may be strategically important within Spatial Sport without itself being a sport.

Computational Contest

A skilled competition whose decisive environment is computational but whose athletic embodiment or Spatial Athletic System membership is absent, insufficiently evidenced, or unresolved. This is a sibling analytical class, not a statement of inferiority.

Performance Agency and embodiment

Human involvement is too broad to define the category. Performance Agency is meaningful in-performance influence by a human performer through skill exercised during the relevant contest or performance window.

  • Pre-contest design and engineering do not establish Performance Agency.
  • Remote control can preserve Performance Agency.
  • Declared assistance can preserve Performance Agency.
  • Autonomous execution can produce an open competition without live human Performance Agency.

Embodiment is not imposed as a universal definition of sport. Chess and other recognized mind sports make that position indefensible without an artificially narrow scope. Embodied Performance is instead treated as a candidate-defining property of the Spatial Athletic System class. No single normative body model is permitted.

Not automatically included

The following are not automatically Spatial Athletic Systems or Spatial Athletic Contests:

  • a game rendered in 3D;
  • a competitive computational arena;
  • conventional esports solely because they are digital;
  • passive immersive sports video;
  • decorative virtual scenery;
  • a betting product labeled “virtual sports”;
  • a physical sport with unrelated overlays;
  • telemetry or measurement assistance alone;
  • a simulation with no contest structure;
  • an autonomous contest with no live human Performance Agency;
  • a provider’s unsupported “spatial” claim.

Falsifiability

The thesis must be revised if later evidence shows that Performance Agency does not distinguish athletic contest from autonomous competition; that embodied performance adds no useful distinction; that operational spatial profiles cannot be tied to observable mechanisms; or that an existing category already performs the same function with greater precision.

A category thesis that cannot be corrected cannot become a reference.
