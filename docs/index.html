Reference infrastructure for Spatial Sport

What becomes of sport when the space that contains it becomes computational?

AltisSports is governed reference infrastructure for describing, inspecting, citing, and reviewing spatial athletic systems across physical, virtual, distributed, human, machine, and hybrid environments. It is not a sports publication, a ranking system, or a certification authority.

Why Altis

The name is structural, not decorative.

The Altis at ancient Olympia was more than a venue for athletic action. It was a bounded civic and sacred environment in which competition, rules, spectatorship, ritual, identity, authority, and collective memory were held together. Sport did not exist independently of the environment that contained it.

Today that environment is changing. Athletic action may pass through physical rooms, computational arenas, sensors, remote participants, adaptive equipment, software rules, automated officiation, machine inference, virtual spectatorship, and distributed networks. The arena can now sense, compute, mediate, constrain, measure, compare, and officiate. That changes the analytical problem.

The ancient Altis contained sport in physical space. AltisSports examines how sport is described, evidenced, and governed when space itself becomes computational.

History is not used here as ornament. It provides the structural analogy from which the reference problem begins. It is not a claim of continuity with ancient institutions, and it does not confer authority by myth.

When space becomes computational

A headset does not create a new sporting category merely because it renders three-dimensional space. A sensor does not make two performances comparable merely because both produce the same metric. A virtual environment does not become an athletic arena merely because competition occurs inside it. Automation does not become human performance merely because a human designed the system.

Immersion is not embodiment

Immersion concerns perceptual presence. Spatiality concerns operational function. High presence may coexist with weak spatial-athletic integration.

Mediation is not spatiality

Vehicles, prostheses, controllers, sensors, and remote interfaces can mediate performance without making the arena computationally constituted.

Technology is not category membership

A product, platform, headset, trainer, or overlay does not inherit Spatial Sport status from branding.

The deeper question is: what operational relationship exists between human performance and computationally mediated space, and what evidence allows that relationship to be inspected?

That is the problem space AltisSports calls Spatial Sport.

The missing reference layer

Spatial Sport is emerging through disconnected products and practices: immersive athletic training, motion-based competition, mixed-reality exercise, remote embodied participation, distributed arenas, sensor-mediated performance, computational officiation, adaptive and assistive systems, spatial broadcasting, and human–machine performance systems.

Products, platforms, competitions, sensors, and research exist. What remains fragmented is the reference layer between them.

Without that layer, simulation can be confused with sport, immersion with embodiment, provider claims with independent fact, measurements with valid comparability, automated execution with live Performance Agency, and coded rules with inspectable constraints. Accessibility can be treated as an edge condition rather than part of system architecture. Institutions can struggle to compare systems whose technical vocabulary is incompatible.

AltisSports is being built to make those distinctions explicit, traceable, and inspectable.

From evidence to reference infrastructure

AltisSports does not begin with a score or a certification scheme. It begins with boundary evidence and progressively builds governed reference objects around it. No layer is allowed to become a separate source of truth.

Boundary Cases — stratified evidence that tests distinctions
First Principles, Ontology, Category Thesis — the conceptual field
AS³ — 10-layer inspection architecture
Descriptive Atlas and Query Surface — what the corpus knows, retrieved without scoring
Stable citation objects — identifiers, claim class, license, temporal status
ASR evidence profiles — unpublished Working Draft, not a published standard
Independent, governed review — claim classes, provenance, correction

How to read the public record

This website is a public reference surface. It presents the asset; it does not become the source of truth.

  • GitHub remains the canonical working reference.
  • Zenodo v0.1 is the frozen citable publication of the conceptual and architectural foundation.
  • Explanatory writing, including on Substack, is commentary. It is not a source of truth and does not outrank the governed repository or the frozen DOI.

Publication of the conceptual foundation does not publish, ratify, validate, or elevate ASR-001. ASR-001 remains an unpublished Working Draft. The citability hold CITE-HOLD-ASR-001 remains open.

How to cite AltisSports · Claim classes and non-claims

What AltisSports does not claim

A reference must survive skeptical inspection. AltisSports is not:

  • a sports news publication
  • a product certification authority
  • a conformance service for underlying systems
  • a sports-recognition body
  • an adopted or recognized standard
  • a universal definition of sport
  • a ranking system, maturity ladder, or spatiality score
  • a safety or clinical standard
  • a claim of industry adoption or external endorsement
  • the owner of the descriptive words “Spatial Sport”
