Provisional reference architecture · v0.3 · not a standard

AS³ — Altis Spatial Sport Stack

AS³ locates where a spatial athletic system enables performance, carries rules, produces measurements, and can lose integrity. It is an inspection architecture, not a maturity model.

Layers are not levels. Their presence does not produce a score. The number of present layers does not determine quality or category membership. AS³ does not authorize a total score, a spatiality level, a maturity ladder, certification, conformance claims, paid rankings, or a single yes/no category badge without an evidence profile.

The classified object must be locked before layer analysis. AS³ may inspect an activity, contest, athletic system, spatial athletic system, support system, experience, product, event, or organization. It does not repair an ambiguous object definition.

Ten inspection layers

The labels L1–L10 are locators, not ranks.

L1

Performance Agency

Who or what performs, and how live human skill affects the relevant performance window. Design contribution must not be confused with in-performance agency.

L2

Performance Interface and Embodied Demand

How human skill enters the system, and what bodily, physiological, cognitive, sensorimotor, adaptive, or assisted channels materially contribute. Mediation does not by itself establish spatiality. No normative body model is authorized by v0.3.

L3

Arena and Spatial State

How physical, computational, unified, and distributed arenas are represented and synchronized, including boundaries, zones, shared state, and failure when physical and computational boundaries diverge.

L4

Constraint and Rule Execution

Where rules reside and how textual, human, equipment, sensor, geometry, or code-based rules become operative. Inspectability, versioning, and divergence between declared and implemented rules are central.

L5

Sensing, Tracking, and State Estimation

How participant, equipment, arena, and event state are observed, measured, estimated, or inferred — including accuracy, latency, drift, occlusion, device inequality, and failure behavior.

L6

Measurement and Comparability

How observations become metrics, and under which conditions comparisons are valid. Missing controls create unresolved evidence, not an automatic score penalty.

L7

Officiation and Outcome

How rule-relevant events become decisions, penalties, rankings, corrections, appeals, or results. An automated result may be open without containing live human Performance Agency.

L8

Presence and Participation

How performers, officials, coaches, spectators, and remote actors perceive, communicate, and affect the system. Presence and immersion are not evidence of Spatial Athletic System membership.

L9

Safety, Accessibility, and Human Limits

How physical, physiological, cognitive, adaptive, and social participation conditions are disclosed. Accessibility is constitutive system design, not an edge-case add-on.

L10

Governance, Evidence, and Change

How authority, provenance, accountability, versioning, correction, data rights, conflicts, and external inspectability are governed.

Cross-layer conditions

Across those layers, AS³ also examines conditions such as integrity, agency handoff, distributed arenas, participatory actors, intentional biological control, comparability, accessibility, privacy, security, and operational spatial integration.

Operational Spatial Integration is a cross-layer condition, not an eleventh layer. For the locked object it records which spatial functions are evidenced: represent, enable, mediate, constrain, measure, compare, officiate. Those descriptors are qualitative research fields. They are not levels, points, weights, or a maturity sequence.

Failure principle

A broken link among agency, interface, arena, rule, sensing, measurement, decision, participation, safety, and governance can invalidate conclusions produced above it. The sequence is analytical, not necessarily chronological.
