# Second Antagonistic Stratum Design — BC-011–BC-020

**Asset:** AltisSports  
**Sprint:** Sprint 4 — Corpus Infrastructure and Second-Stratum Readiness  
**Status:** Ratified Research Design — Cases Not Yet Analyzed  
**Target analysis sprint:** Sprint 5  
**Schema:** `BOUNDARY_CASE_SCHEMA_V0.2.json`

## 1. Purpose

The second stratum must test the corrections introduced by Sprint 3 rather than repeat the first sample with different brands or sports.

The stratum targets:

- low-locomotion embodied performance;
- computational arenas with stronger bodily channels;
- distributed physical–computational arenas;
- support systems that resemble sport;
- assisted and alternative agency channels;
- human–AI agency handoff;
- spectator influence on athletic outcomes.

## 2. Selection Rule

Cases were selected because each can falsify or sharpen at least one of the following:

- Performance Agency;
- the provisional Spatial Athletic System class;
- Embodied Performance;
- Operational Spatial Integration functions;
- object-lock discipline;
- the Computational Contest sibling class;
- the Spatial Support System class;
- AS³ cross-layer conditions.

Selection does not imply category membership.

## 4. Case Hypotheses and Falsifiers

### BC-011 — Competitive Shooting

**Pressure:** Gross locomotion is limited, but bodily stability, sensorimotor control, timing, and precision are decisive.

**Tests:**

- whether Embodied Performance can be defined without equating it with locomotion or exertion;
- whether physical rule-bearing space can remain non-spatial under Altis terminology;
- whether measurement precision creates comparability without computational arena integration.

**Falsifier:** If the current embodiment vocabulary cannot distinguish shooting from non-embodied computational input, L2 requires revision.

### BC-012 — Cue Sports

**Pressure:** Fine motor performance, physical geometry, and low physiological demand.

**Tests:**

- physical spatial reasoning versus computational Spatiality;
- consequence and contest structure under low exertion;
- whether the system remains outside the Spatial Athletic core despite high spatial skill.

**Falsifier:** If “spatial skill” is repeatedly confused with Operational Spatial Integration, category language requires an explicit semantic warning.

### BC-013 — VR Rhythm Competition

**Pressure:** Tracked movement and a computationally constituted arena may be strong while sport governance and comparability remain weak.

**Tests:**

- whether strong embodiment plus computational constraint is sufficient for Spatial Athletic System membership;
- whether Spatial Athletic Contest membership still requires stronger contest integrity;
- patch, tracking, hardware, and scoring comparability.

**Falsifier:** If the framework cannot separate system membership from contest maturity without inventing a score, object classes need refinement.

### BC-014 — Location-Based Free-Roam VR Competition

**Pressure:** Physical movement and computational geometry are jointly operational.

**Tests:**

- boundary alignment;
- tracking volume;
- collision risk;
- rule-bearing zones;
- local multiplayer synchronization;
- whether Operational Spatial Integration can be evidenced as constitutive.

**Falsifier:** If the profile cannot identify the failure effect of physical–computational divergence, the spatial-function model is insufficient.

### BC-015 — Remote Motion-Tracked Martial Competition

**Pressure:** Human performers occupy different physical spaces while sharing a computational contest state.

**Tests:**

- remote Performance Agency;
- latency and calibration;
- distributed arena equivalence;
- virtual contact and officiation;
- safety when physical consequences and represented contact diverge.

**Falsifier:** If local and distributed arenas need different integrity primitives, AS³ L3/L5/L6/L7 require sharper relations.

### BC-016 — Rehabilitation Exercise Game

**Pressure:** Embodied movement and computational feedback may be strong without sport or open contest structure.

**Tests:**

- Spatial Support System classification;
- object lock among product, therapy protocol, experience, and game;
- provider effectiveness claims;
- safety and clinical evidence boundaries.

**Falsifier:** If the category still tends to label a spatially embodied support product as sport, the field/core-class distinction has failed.

### BC-017 — Exoskeleton-Supported Competition

**Pressure:** Real-time assistance alters bodily capacity and may share causal contribution with the performer.

**Tests:**

- Assisted Performance Agency;
- agency attribution;
- equipment classes;
- adaptive access versus performance substitution;
- inspectability of assistance.

**Falsifier:** If agency cannot be described without declaring assistance either irrelevant or disqualifying, the agency model needs additional subtypes.

### BC-018 — Brain-Computer-Interface Competition

**Pressure:** Human intention may directly control a system with little conventional motor output.

**Tests:**

- causal Performance Agency;
- whether bodily signal generation counts as Embodied Performance;
- cognitive and physiological channels;
- calibration and signal variability;
- computational contest versus Spatial Athletic System.

**Falsifier:** If embodiment is reduced to visible movement, v0.2 is too narrow. If every biological signal is enough, it may be too broad.

### BC-019 — Mixed Human–AI Team Competition

**Pressure:** Performance authority may shift among humans and AI during the same contest.

**Tests:**

- Agency Handoff;
- attribution of decisions;
- declared assistance;
- autonomous execution periods;
- outcome openness;
- comparison between different assistance regimes.

**Falsifier:** If one record cannot represent time-varying agency, the schema requires event- or phase-level agency segments.

### BC-020 — Spectator-Influenced Athletic Competition

**Pressure:** Spectators may vote, trigger modifiers, alter resources, or change constraints.

**Tests:**

- when spectators become performers or rule actors;
- whether L8 influence becomes constitutive;
- fairness and information asymmetry;
- contest integrity under external intervention.

**Falsifier:** If the ontology cannot distinguish spectator participation from performing agency and officiation, role relations require revision.

## 5. Evidence Minimum

Each case must use at least:

1. one current primary rule, institutional, technical, or protocol source;
2. one additional source supporting a distinct material field;
3. explicit provider-claim labeling where independent evidence is absent;
4. access dates and temporal status;
5. a direct statement of evidence limitations.

Clinical or effectiveness claims in BC-016 require research or authoritative health evidence and must not be inferred from product marketing.

## 6. Analysis Order

For each case:

1. lock the primary classified object;
2. define the performance window;
3. identify Performance Agency and any handoff;
4. describe interface and Embodied Demand;
5. map AS³ L1–L10;
6. create the Operational Spatial Integration Profile;
7. test the four candidate contest invariants;
8. record Consequence Structure separately;
9. state sport/contest axis and category relation;
10. document objections, uncertainty, and falsifiers;
11. validate the machine record against schema v0.2.

## 7. Cross-Case Questions

The final Sprint 5 findings must answer:

- Does Embodied Performance need subtypes beyond demand lists?
- Is a minimum embodiment condition defensible for Spatial Athletic Systems?
- Does Agency Handoff require time-segmented records?
- Can Operational Spatial Integration functions remain qualitative and still discriminate usefully?
- Are Spatial Support System and Computational Contest sufficient sibling classes?
- Does distributed space require a dedicated arena relation?
- When does spectator influence become Performance Agency, rule execution, or external interference?
- Does any case justify restoring Consequence as an independent invariant?

## 8. Prohibited Shortcuts

Sprint 5 must not:

- use one commercial product as proof of an entire activity class;
- infer sport status from branding;
- treat high immersion as high spatial-athletic integration;
- convert unknown evidence into absence;
- average AS³ layers;
- rank vendors;
- issue ASR conformance;
- replace a selected case because it is inconvenient.

## 9. Exit Criteria for Sprint 5

Sprint 5 may pass only when:

- ten human-readable analyses are complete;
- ten native v0.2 JSON records validate;
- every record identifies a primary object and performance window;
- every Spatial Function has evidence or explicit unresolved status;
- cross-case findings state which v0.2 propositions survive, fail, or require revision;
- migration-derived BC-001–BC-010 fields are not confused with native v0.2 records;
- `ASR-001` remains on hold unless a separate gate later authorizes scope drafting.
